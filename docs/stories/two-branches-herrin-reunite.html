Two Branches of the Herrin Family Reunite

Family History

Author: Family Archive Research

A Family Takes Root in Georgia

In the middle of the nineteenth century, southeastern Georgia was home to a growing family whose descendants would eventually follow two different paths before coming together again generations later. At the center of this story were Levi Herrin, born around 1804, and his wife, Charity Sears. By 1850, Levi and Charity were living in Ware County, Georgia, where the federal census recorded a large household that included William Herring, age 19, and Charlotte Herring, age 13. Their surname appeared as Herring in the census, one of the spelling variations found in historical records of the family. William and Charlotte would become central figures in two branches of the Herrin family. William's branch would eventually lead to his grandson, William Levi Herrin. Charlotte's branch would lead to her granddaughter, Laura Etta Palmer. Generations later, those two descendants would marry, bringing the family lines together again.

William M. Herrin and Mary Hargraves

On January 3, 1853, William M. Herrin married Mary Hargraves in Ware County, Georgia. Mary was the daughter of Thomas Hopewell Hargraves and Nancy Beverly. Like William, she had been living in Ware County when the 1850 federal census was taken. Interestingly, their families appeared just twelve household numbers apart in that census. Although this does not establish how close their homes were, it places both families in the same census enumeration area several years before their marriage. On May 22, 1854, their son Thomas Levi Herrin was born. The family continued to grow, and by 1870 the census recorded William and Mary living in Ware County with Thomas and several other children. Thomas later married Nancy T. Jewell. Their son, William Levi Herrin, was born on May 4, 1892, in Homerville, Georgia. Through William Levi, the first branch of our story was approaching the generation in which the family lines would meet again.

Charlotte Herrin and the Williams Family

While William M. Herrin was establishing his family with Mary Hargraves, another branch of the Herrin family was beginning to take shape. William's younger sister, Charlotte Herrin, was born around 1837 in Georgia. She appears in the 1850 federal census living in the household of Levi and Charity Herrin in Ware County. Around 1854, Charlotte married Hezekiah Ponder Williams in Clinch County, Georgia. Hezekiah, born in 1828, was the son of John Williams Sr. and Nancy Smith. On April 29, 1855, their daughter Laura Williams was born. Laura later married Henry Palmer, and on June 25, 1898, their daughter Laura Etta was born in Pearson, Georgia. Laura Etta's name appears in historical records as Lauretta, Laura, Laura E and Laura Etta. Through her grandmother Charlotte, Laura Etta shared ancestry with William Levi Herrin, the grandson of Charlotte's brother, William M. Herrin.

Two Branches Reunited

In April 1916, in Clinch County, Georgia, William Levi Herrin and Laura Etta Palmer were married. Their marriage represented more than the beginning of a new family. It brought together two branches of the Herrin family that had descended from the same ancestors several generations earlier. William Levi traced his ancestry through Thomas Levi Herrin and William M. Herrin to Levi Herrin and Charity Sears. Laura Etta traced her ancestry through Laura Williams and Charlotte Herrin to the same couple. According to the reconstructed family lineage, William Levi and Laura Etta were third cousins. Whether they knew about their shared ancestry when they married is unknown. Their descendants would carry forward the history of both branches, including their daughter Ollie Mae Herrin, born September 6, 1917, in Homerville, Georgia. Their story reminds us that family history is more than a collection of names and dates. It is the story of generations whose lives intersect in ways they may never have fully understood.

Sources and Research Notes

The 1850 United States census for Ware County, Georgia, records William and Charlotte Herring in the household of Levi and Charity Herring. The 1870 census records William Herring, Mary Herring, and Thomas L. Herring in the same household. The marriage of William M. Herrin and Mary Hargraves on January 3, 1853, and the marriage of William Levi Herrin and Laura Etta Palmer in April 1916 are dates provided through family research. An original marriage document for the 1916 marriage has not yet been obtained. Some parent-child relationships in the reconstructed lineage require additional independent documentation.
