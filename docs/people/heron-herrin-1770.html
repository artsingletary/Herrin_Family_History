Biography

Heron Herrin was born around 1770 in North Carolina. Family research identifies him by the nickname Mack, although contemporary documentation confirming this name has not yet been located.

In September 1799, a land warrant was issued for 400 acres in Glynn County, Georgia, to a man recorded as Heron Herron. A corresponding land grant was issued in 1800. These records are consistent with the account preserved in family research.

According to research compiled by Arthur Singletary Sr., Heron moved to Camden County, Georgia, around 1811 and worked as a farm overseer for Burwell Atkinson. The research states that Atkinson killed Heron during an argument in 1817 and was subsequently tried for murder. The original court proceedings have not yet been examined.

Heron and his wife Ann reportedly had seven children: Olive, John, Nancy, James, Levi, William, and Daniel Herrin.

Family research suggests that Heron may have been the son of Enoch Herin, but this relationship remains unverified.

  1. 1821 Georgia Land Lottery — Herron Herron's Orphans
    Type: Land

    The Georgia Land Lottery Register records 'Herron Herron's orps' among the fortunate drawers on November 28, 1821. The entry identifies Lot 175 in the 10th Land District of Houston County, Georgia. This contemporary record establishes that children identified as orphans of Herron Herron received a land lottery allotment. It corresponds with family research concerning Heron Herrin (1770–1817), although the children are not individually named.
