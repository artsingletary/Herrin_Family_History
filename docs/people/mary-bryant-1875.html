Biography

Mary Evline Bryant was born in May 1875 in Florida, the daughter of John Waters Bryan (1829–1878) and Dolly Ann Leslie (born 1834). Her surname appears as both Bryan and Bryant in historical and family research records. Her middle name is inscribed as Evline on her gravestone, although the spelling has not been independently confirmed by a contemporary record.

Mary was the youngest of six known children. Her siblings were John Bryant (1854–1928), Amanda M. Bryan (born 1856), Myram D. Bryan (1859–1916), Alexander Eugene Bryan (1869–1901), and Hattie Bryan (1871–1885). Her father died in 1878, when Mary was approximately three years old.

In the 1880 United States Federal Census, five-year-old Mary was living in Columbia County, Florida, with her widowed mother, Dolly Bryant, and siblings Myram, Alexander, and Hattie. The census provides an early record of Mary's childhood and the family's circumstances following her father's death.

On February 4, 1890, Mary Bryant married John V. Singletary in Lafayette County, Florida. Their marriage license was issued on February 3, 1890, and the ceremony was performed by Rev. A. T. Wood. The marriage was recorded on March 3, 1890, in Lafayette County Marriage Record Book B, page 422. Mary was fourteen years old at the time of her marriage.

Mary and John established their family in Lafayette County, Florida, where John worked as a farmer. Their six known children were Jessie Von Singletary (born 1891), Annie Evaline Singletary (1893–1951), George William Singletary Sr. (1895–1964), Albert Eugene Singletary (1899–1928), Alice Geneva Singletary (1904–1928), and Viola Singletary (1906–1987). Family research suggests that Jessie died young, possibly during the 1910s, although his death date remains unverified.

The 1900 United States Federal Census recorded John and Mary living in Lafayette County with their four oldest children: Jessie, Annie, George, and Albert. Mary was recorded as twenty-five years old, born in May 1875. The couple reported ten years of marriage, and Mary reported having given birth to four children, all four of whom were living.

By the 1910 United States Federal Census, John and Mary's household included Annie, George, Albert, Alice, and Viola. Jessie, then approximately eighteen or nineteen years old, was no longer living in the household. Mary reported having given birth to six children, all six of whom were living. John continued to work as a farmer in Lafayette County.

Mary and John were both living when the 1910 census was recorded. Their exact death dates have not been independently verified. Neither has yet been positively identified in the 1920 census records examined during this research.

Mary Evline Bryant Singletary and John Vershal Singletary are buried at Bethel Creek Baptist Church Cemetery, formerly known as Bell's Mill Creek Cemetery, in Day, Lafayette County, Florida. Their original grave markers remain at the cemetery.

In 2011, their great-grandson Arthur Singletary installed an additional shared headstone commemorating John and Mary. The death dates inscribed on the newer marker reflect family research and have not been independently verified. Their original grave markers, along with the original marker of their son Jessie Von Singletary, provide additional evidence of the family's burial history.
