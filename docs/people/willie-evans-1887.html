Biography

Willie Evans, also recorded as Will Evans, was born on July 9, 1887, according to the dates inscribed on his gravestone in Live Oak Cemetery, Live Oak, Florida. His birthplace and the identities of his parents have not yet been conclusively established. Federal census records provide differing information about his birthplace, identifying Florida in some years and Georgia in 1930.

On October 24, 1915, Willie married Emma Singletary (1895–1984), daughter of Reverend Jacob A. Singletary and Ida Alla Miller Singletary, in Lafayette County, Florida. Their marriage license was issued on October 23, 1915, in Mayo, and the marriage was recorded on November 13 in Marriage Record Book E, page 166. The marriage record identifies him as Will Evans.

By January 1920, Willie and Emma were living in Precinct 8 of Suwannee County, Florida, in the Dowling Park area. The federal census recorded Willie, age 32, as head of household and employed in a sawmill. Emma and their infant son Ernest Evans, approximately three months old, were also enumerated in the household.

Dowling Park was an important center of the northern Florida lumber industry during the early twentieth century. Sawmills, timber operations, and railroad connections shaped the community and provided employment for local families. Willie's occupation placed him within this regional lumber economy, although the available records do not identify his specific employer.

In April 1930, Willie and Emma continued to live in the Dowling Park area of Suwannee County. The census recorded Willie, age 42, working as a laborer in a shingle or lath mill. Their children Ernest and Gracie were living with them. Emma's younger brother Jasper Singletary and his wife were enumerated in the preceding household, suggesting that the two families were close neighbors. The Evans family was recorded as renting its residence.

By April 1940, Willie and Emma had moved to Mayo in Lafayette County, Florida. The federal census recorded Willie E. Evans, age 52, as head of household. His wife Emma, their sons Ernest and Frederick, and their daughter Gracie were living with him. Also in the household was Gracie's husband, Owen Lassiter, identified as Willie's son-in-law. This census provides a particularly useful record of the family together during their adult children's early years.

In April 1950, Willie and Emma were living in Suwannee County, Florida. Willie, age 62, was employed as a watchman at a wholesale planing mill, continuing his long association with the lumber industry. Emma, age 54, was listed as his wife, and no children or other relatives were enumerated in their household. The couple reported that they had lived in the same house one year earlier.

Willie and Emma had at least three children: Ernest Evans, Frederick Evans, also known as Fred, and Gracie Evans Lassiter, also known as Grace. The family's census records trace their lives between Lafayette and Suwannee counties and document Willie's employment in sawmill and lumber-related occupations over several decades.

Willie Evans died on October 7, 1971, at the age of 84. His obituary, published in The Florida Times-Union on October 9, 1971, identified his surviving wife, Emma; daughter Grace Lassiter of Palatka; and sons Ernest and Fred Evans of Jacksonville. He was also survived by four grandchildren and seven great-grandchildren. Funeral services were scheduled for October 9, 1971, at Westwood Baptist Church in Live Oak, Florida, with Harris Funeral Home handling the arrangements. Willie was buried in Live Oak Cemetery, where he shares a gravestone with Emma, who died in 1984.
