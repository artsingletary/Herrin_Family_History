Biography

Sarah R. Singletary was born in South Carolina around 1840. She was enumerated in the 1850 United States Federal Census in Sumter District, South Carolina, in the household of Jacob and Eliza B. Singletary. The census recorded her as ten years old.

By 1860, Sarah had moved with the Singletary household to Decatur County, Georgia. The federal census recorded her as nineteen years old.

In 1870, Sarah was enumerated in District 720 of Decatur County, Georgia, in the household of Jacob Singletary. The census recorded her name as Sarah A. Singletarry, age twenty-five, born in South Carolina. Earlier census records identified her as Sarah R. Singletary. The variation in her middle initial remains unresolved, although her 1890 marriage record later identified her as S. R. A. Singletary.

On January 30, 1890, Sarah married William Johnson in Lafayette County, Florida. Their marriage license was issued on January 28, and the ceremony was performed by Elder J. A. Singletary, believed to be Sarah's brother, Reverend Jacob A. Singletary. The marriage was officially recorded on February 3, 1890. The original marriage record identifies the bride as Miss S. R. A. Singletary, providing additional evidence of her middle initials. Although she is identified as Sarah Ann Singletary in FamilySearch, her full middle names have not yet been independently established.

In 1900, Sarah was living in Lafayette County, Florida, with her husband, William Johnson. The federal census recorded her birth as March 1840 and her age as sixty. Her mother, Eliza Singletary, and sister, Mary Singletary, were also members of the household. Sarah was recorded as having given birth to no children, with none living. Interestingly, both Sarah and William were recorded as having been married for thirty years, suggesting a marriage around 1870. However, their original marriage record documents a ceremony on January 30, 1890. The reason for this discrepancy remains unresolved.

Sarah died on October 31, 1907, in Day, Lafayette County, Florida. Her death certificate records her birth year as 1840, identifies her husband as William Johnson, and gives her father's surname as Singletary. The certificate lists paralysis of the heart as the cause of death. Her husband, William Johnson, served as the informant, establishing that he was still living at the time of her death. The certificate records her burial location as Day, Florida, but does not identify a cemetery. Bethel Creek Baptist Church Cemetery, where her mother Eliza Singletary is buried, is a possible burial location, although this has not been confirmed.
