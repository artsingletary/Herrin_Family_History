Biography

Hope Wayland Sullivan was born on September 12, 1920, in Mayo, Lafayette County, Florida. He was the son of John M. Sullivan and Arzada Boyd Sullivan, as identified on his gravestone. His World War II draft registration also identifies John M. Sullivan as his employer and the person who would always know his address in February 1942.

On February 15, 1942, Hope registered for the World War II draft in Mayo, Florida. He was 21 years old and lived on Route 2 in Mayo. His registration card identified John M. Sullivan as both his employer and the person who would always know his address. The card described Hope as five feet eight inches tall, weighing 118 pounds, with blue eyes, brown hair, and a ruddy complexion. His registration does not, by itself, establish military service.

On April 17, 1942, Hope married Ida Mae Herrin in Lafayette County, Florida. Their marriage license was issued that day in Mayo, and the certificate records that they were married at a private home. The marriage was recorded on April 18, 1942.

Hope and Ida Mae had a family that included several children with the Sullivan surname. Hope's obituary identifies seven surviving children: Nell Slaughter, Connie Mack Sullivan, Billy John Sullivan, Wayland Sullivan, Paul (Mike) Sullivan, Drew Sullivan, and Jack Sullivan. Ida Mae's later gravestone also names Stella S. Osborn, whose relationship to Hope has not yet been independently established.

Hope later lived in Perry, Taylor County, Florida, where he worked as a lineman for an electrical company. According to his obituary, he had lived in Perry for approximately five years before his death.

Hope died on May 27, 1982, in Taylor County, Florida, at the age of 61. At the time of his death, he was living in Perry and working as a lineman for an electrical company. His funeral was held at Airline Baptist Church in Mayo, Florida, with burial in the church cemetery. His gravestones commemorate his life, identify his parents as John M. and Arzada Boyd Sullivan, and include the inscription 'Daddy' and 'Gone But Not Forgotten.'
