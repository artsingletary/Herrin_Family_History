Biography

Fred Evans Sr. was born on May 21, 1924, in Dowling Park, Suwannee County, Florida, the son of Willie Evans and Emma Singletary Evans. His maternal grandparents were Reverend Jacob A. Singletary and Ida Alla Miller Singletary. His birth date is supported by his gravestone and Social Security records, although his 1942 draft registration records May 3, 1924, and a military service index records May 1, 1924.

The 1940 United States federal census recorded Fred, age 16, living with his parents in Mayo, Lafayette County, Florida. Also in the household were his older brother Ernest Evans, his sister Gracie Lassiter, and his brother-in-law Owen Lassiter.

On June 30, 1942, shortly after his eighteenth birthday, Fred registered for the World War II draft in Live Oak, Suwannee County, Florida. His registration card listed his residence as General Delivery, Live Oak, and identified his mother, Mrs. Emma Evans, as the person who would always know his address. He was employed by Dan Sanders, with his place of employment listed as Madison Highway in Live Oak. The card described him as five feet nine and one-half inches tall, weighing 135 pounds, with blue eyes, blond hair, and a light complexion.

During World War II, Fred served in the United States Army. His Army enlistment record indicates that he enlisted on March 11, 1943, at Camp Blanding, Florida. At the time, he was single without dependents, had a grammar school education, and his civilian occupation was classified as a semiskilled chauffeur or driver. He entered military service as a private and was later identified as a Technician Fifth Grade (TEC 5), according to his military gravestone. A separate military service index lists an enlistment date of March 18, 1943, and a discharge date of January 18, 1946. His specific unit, assignments, and locations of military service have not yet been established.

The 1945 Florida State Population Census recorded Fred, age 21, in the household of his parents, Willie and Emma Evans, in Precinct 1, Live Oak, Suwannee County, Florida. Other household members included his brother Ernest, Elizabeth Evans, his sister Grace Lassiter, her husband Owen Lassiter, and their young son Owen Jr. Elizabeth may have been Ernest's wife, although that relationship has not yet been confirmed. Because Fred was serving in the Army during this period, his enumeration does not necessarily establish that he was physically present in the household when the census was taken.

By April 1950, Fred was married to Marie Richmond Evans. The couple lived in Suwannee County, Florida, with four children: Charles Richmond, age 10; Donald Richmond, age 8; Freddie Evans, age 2; and Willie Evans, age 1. The 1950 federal census identified Charles and Donald as Fred's stepsons. Their biological father has not yet been identified, and it is not known whether Richmond was Marie's maiden name or a surname from an earlier marriage.

On June 7, 1952, Fred and his family were enjoying an outing along the Suwannee River near Suwannee Springs, Florida. According to contemporary newspaper reports, Donald Ray entered the river and was caught in a powerful undertow. His older brother Charles attempted to rescue him, followed by their mother Marie. All three drowned. Fred also entered the river in an attempt to save his family and was swept into the current. His brother-in-law, Owen Lassiter, managed to rescue Fred, although he was unable to save Marie and the two boys. The newspaper reports identified the boys as Charles and Donald Ray Evans, while the 1950 census recorded their surname as Richmond.

Fred survived the tragedy and continued living in Florida. He was identified as a surviving son residing in Jacksonville in the 1971 obituary of his father, Willie Evans, and again in the 1984 obituary of his mother, Emma Singletary Evans.

Fred Evans Sr. died on July 26, 1993, at the age of 69. He was buried in Live Oak Cemetery in Live Oak, Suwannee County, Florida. His military gravestone commemorates his World War II service in the United States Army and identifies his rank as Technician Fifth Grade. His exact place of death has not yet been established.
