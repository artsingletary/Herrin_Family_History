Biography

David Sears was born in North Carolina in 1791, the son of John Sears, a Revolutionary War soldier. According to Pioneers of Wiregrass Georgia, his father moved the family to Montgomery County, Georgia, around 1795 or 1796.

David married Levicy Nobles on April 19, 1810, in Montgomery County, Georgia. Levicy was the daughter of Lewis Sanders Nobles, who also served during the Revolutionary War.

David and Levicy raised thirteen children, including Charity Sears (1812–1898), who married Levi Herrin. Through Charity, they became ancestors of both branches of the Herrin family featured in the story Two Branches of the Herrin Family Reunite.

On June 20, 1809, David was commissioned an ensign in the 52nd District of Emanuel County. During the War of 1812, he served as a private in Captain John G. Colbert's company, 2nd Regiment of Georgia Militia. He enlisted on October 12, 1814, and was discharged on March 15, 1815, according to the published family history.

Around 1825, David moved his family from Montgomery County to what was then Ware County, Georgia, settling in the 586th Georgia Militia District. This territory subsequently became part of Clinch, Coffee, and Atkinson counties.

The published family history references David in the 1820 Montgomery County census, the 1830, 1840, and 1850 Ware County censuses, and the 1860 Coffee County census.

David died in 1879. He and Levicy were buried at Union Hill Congregational Church cemetery, where they were members.
